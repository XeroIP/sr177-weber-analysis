Section 04 — Noise, Environment & Inland Port

Acoustic Environment, Noise Policy & Economic Implications

Noise Abatement Policy

FHWA Noise Criteria and Sound Wall Eligibility

UDOT's noise policy is governed by the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) Noise Abatement Criteria (NAC) and Utah Administrative Code R930-3. A traffic noise impact is officially identified when predicted noise levels equal or exceed 66 decibels (dBA) for residential areas (Activity Category B).

When an impact is identified, UDOT evaluates mitigation measures — primarily sound walls — against two criteria:

  • Feasibility: The wall must achieve a minimum 5 dBA noise reduction for impacted receivers.
  • Reasonableness: The wall must be cost-effective (typically evaluated per-benefited-residence) and must receive majority approval through a community balloting process.

Residents within approximately 500 feet of the highway centerline are typically evaluated for noise impacts under current UDOT practice. At a 5100 West centerline location, east-side properties at your address would fall within or very near this evaluation zone.

Sound wall precedent (Phase 2 Davis County): The noise wall balloting process for the Phase 2 extension produced approved walls at multiple locations, establishing a strong local precedent for mitigation at similar distances. All three walls that went to ballot were approved.

Indirect Impacts

Property Value Considerations

Research on property value impacts from freeway construction in comparable Western U.S. suburban markets shows a nuanced picture:

  • Direct adjacency (within 300 ft): Properties directly adjacent to a limited-access freeway typically see value suppression of 5–15%, primarily driven by noise, visual intrusion, and reduced appeal to buyers who prioritize quiet.
  • Sound wall mitigation: Properties behind approved sound walls recover a portion of this impact, often seeing values stabilize closer to neighborhood norms.
  • Accessibility premium: Properties within 1–2 miles of a new interchange frequently see value increases as commute times drop and area desirability rises — often offsetting or exceeding the adjacency discount for properties not directly affected.
  • Uncertainty discount: Properties in a study corridor frequently experience a "planning blight" discount during the period between study announcement and construction — driven by buyer reluctance and difficulty obtaining certain financing. This effect resolves once alignment is finalized.
The Inland Port Nexus

SR-177 and the Western Weber Inland Port

The relationship between SR-177 and the Western Weber Inland Port is a significant and contested dimension of this project. UDOT frames the highway primarily as a commuter corridor serving the 40%+ of western Weber County workers who commute to other counties. However, the highway's design — 65 mph limited-access, four lanes, grade-separated interchanges — is equally capable of handling heavy truck freight.

Local opposition perspective: Critics in Hooper have argued that the highway's true purpose is to funnel semitruck traffic through residential communities to the proposed Inland Port. A letter published in the Standard-Examiner (April 24, 2026) stated directly: "I believe the true purpose of this project is to lay the infrastructure to eventually funnel large amounts of semitruck traffic through our city to the proposed Inland Port."

WFRC's 2023–2050 Regional Transportation Plan explicitly links SR-177 to the "Wasatch Choice Vision" framework, which coordinates transportation with land-use and economic development — including industrial expansion in western Weber County. Whether the freight dimension is a primary design driver or an incidental capability remains an open question in the public record.

The practical implication for residents near 4000 South: if the inland port relationship is substantive, the 4000 South interchange may ultimately carry more heavy truck traffic than a purely commuter-focused design would suggest — with consequences for noise levels, road wear on local arterials, and the character of the 4000 South interchange area.

Section 03 — Engineering

Infrastructure Integration & Design Standards

Design Specifications

Highway Design Standards

SR-177 is engineered to meet the demands of a 65 mph limited-access highway incorporating "Dark Sky" compliant lighting and noise-reducing pavement. The Weber County extension is being planned to the same design standards as the completed Davis County segment, with the 250-foot ROW footprint accommodating four travel lanes, a median, and multi-use trail corridors on both sides.

4000 South Terminus

Interchange Configuration at 4000 South

The 4000 South endpoint represents the southern terminus of the Weber County extension segment. Final traffic modeling will determine whether this terminus is configured as a grade-separated interchange (overpass/underpass with ramps) or an at-grade signalized intersection.

For property owners at 3800 South — approximately two blocks north — this is a significant variable. A full grade-separated interchange typically consumes 15–25 acres of land and generates substantial "feeder traffic" on adjacent local streets. An at-grade intersection has a smaller footprint but limited long-term capacity.

4000 South proximity note: Being at 3800 South places you approximately 1,300 feet north of what could become a major interchange. This is within the zone where construction activity, noise, and increased local arterial traffic will be felt regardless of which interchange configuration is selected.

Geotechnical Challenges

Soft Soils and High Groundwater

The soft soils and high groundwater levels characteristic of western Weber County — the former lakebed environment near the Great Salt Lake — necessitate advanced geotechnical engineering. For bridges designed in earlier phases, UDOT utilized integral bents: supports that distribute forces across the structure rather than concentrating them at discrete footings.

Construction in this segment is a multi-phase process including:

  • Site preparation and clearing
  • Drainage installation (coordinated with the Hooper Water Improvement District)
  • Foundation engineering for soft-soil conditions
  • Infrastructure relocation (Rocky Mountain Power, Davis & Weber Counties Canal Company, Hooper Irrigation Company)
  • Paving and surface work

The Phase 2 Davis County extension provides direct precedent: utility relocations began December 2025, with full construction launching Summer 2026 and a project completion target of late 2028.

Multi-Modal Connections

Trail Extension and Active Transportation

The project includes the extension of the Emigrant Trail from West Point through to the Weber County line, eventually connecting to existing trails on 5500 West. The Phase 2 Davis County extension will extend the trail from 1300 North to connect to the existing 5500 West trail in Weber County.

West Haven City's Master Park Plan (Resolution 09-2026) aims to leverage these trail investments to enhance local connectivity and recreational access. For residents near 3800 South, this trail infrastructure represents the first visible on-the-ground indicator of the corridor's eventual arrival in Weber County.

Near-term visibility: Preliminary utility work from Rocky Mountain Power and Hooper Water is expected along the southern edge of West Haven beginning in the coming months (Summer 2026). This is infrastructure relocation for Phase 2 — not construction of the Weber County segment itself — but it signals the corridor's northward progression.

# Technical Analysis of the SR-177 Weber County Extension: Planning Context, Property Impacts, and Infrastructure Integration at the 5100 West and 3800 South Corridor
The expansion of State Route 177 (SR-177), commonly referred to as the West Davis Highway, represents a seminal shift in the multi-modal transportation architecture of the Wasatch Front. As of April 2026, the project has transitioned from a localized congestion-relief initiative into a comprehensive regional corridor that bridges the socio-economic and logistical divide between northern Davis County and western Weber County. The specific segment extending from 1800 North in West Point toward the 4000 South vicinity in West Haven—traversing the 5100 West and 3800 South areas—functions as the critical nexus for future urban growth, industrial logistics, and community connectivity. This report provides an exhaustive examination of the planning framework, the specific impacts on private and public property, the engineering design standards, and the chronological progression of the project within the context of Utah’s long-range transportation goals.
## Strategic Planning Framework and Regional Context
The planning context for the SR-177 extension is governed by a tiered hierarchy of state and regional mandates, primarily channeled through the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) and the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC). The overarching objective is to accommodate a projected population surge in western Davis and Weber counties, where the number of households is anticipated to increase by 74% by the year 2050. This demographic pressure necessitates a high-capacity, limited-access arterial capable of diverting traffic from the increasingly saturated Interstate 15 (I-15) corridor.
The current State Environmental Study (SES) for the segment between 1800 North and 4000 South is a direct response to these projections. Unlike the earlier 2017 Environmental Impact Statement (EIS), which initially proposed more modest configurations, the 2026 planning cycle recognizes that the accelerated pace of development has rendered previous models obsolete. Consequently, the planning for the Weber County extension is being executed with a 250-foot standard footprint, designed to accommodate four lanes of travel—two in each direction—separated by a median and flanked by multi-use trails.

The integration of SR-177 into the WFRC 2023-2050 Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) ensures that the project aligns with the "Wasatch Choice Vision," which emphasizes the coordination of transportation infrastructure with land-use decisions and economic development strategies. For West Haven and Hooper, this means the highway is not merely a transit route but a catalyst for high-density residential rezoning and industrial expansion, specifically in relation to the Western Weber Inland Port project area.
## The State Environmental Study (SES) and Alternatives Development
The transition of SR-177 into Weber County is currently managed through a State Environmental Study (SES), a rigorous analytical process designed to evaluate the physical, biological, and social impacts of the proposed extension. As of the Spring 2026 reporting period, the study has moved from the initial scoping phase into the "Alternatives Development and Evaluation" stage. This stage is critical for property owners near 5100 West and 3800 South, as it determines the precise alignment of the "Build Alternative" and the configuration of the 4000 South terminus.
The SES study specifically examines the intersection of the highway footprint with existing residential neighborhoods and the unique agricultural heritage of the Hooper area. As of April 2026, city officials confirmed that UDOT has settled on an alignment along 5100 West, where the existing road will likely be converted to function as a frontage road for the corridor.

## Property Impacts and Land Use Transformations
The physical footprint of SR-177 near 5100 West and 3800 South necessitates a significant reallocation of land resources. For the Phase 2 extension in Davis County, re-evaluations conducted in 2024 and 2025 revealed that design refinements resulted in an expanded project footprint compared to the 2017 estimates.
### Residential and Agricultural Displacement
The transition from a two-lane to a four-lane configuration has historically increased the impact on residential and agricultural parcels. In the segment immediately south of the Weber County line, agricultural impacts rose to 516 acres, while residential impacts expanded to 142 acres. Relocation assistance is a primary component of UDOT’s acquisition strategy, providing just compensation based on fair market value. Initial corridor preservation efforts suggest a significant number of properties near 3800 South and 5100 West have already been identified for future acquisition.

## Infrastructure Integration and Technical Design Standards
The engineering of SR-177 is designed to meet the demands of a 65 mph limited-access highway while incorporating "Dark Sky" compliant lighting and noise-reducing pavement. The 5100 West alignment is expected to feature high-capacity interchanges or at-grade intersections depending on final traffic modeling for the 4000 South terminus.
### Geotechnical Considerations and Structural Design
The soft soils and high groundwater levels in western Weber County necessitate advanced geotechnical engineering. For the bridges designed in earlier phases, UDOT utilized "integral bents"—supports that distribute forces across the structure. Construction is a multi-phase process including site preparation, drainage installation (coordinated with the Hooper Water Improvement District), and paving.
### Multi-Modal and Trail Connections
The project includes the extension of the Emigrant Trail from West Point through to the Weber County line, eventually connecting to existing trails on 5500 West. For West Haven, the "Master Park Plan" (Resolution 09-2026) aims to leverage these trail investments to enhance local connectivity.
## Acoustic Environment and Noise Abatement Policy
UDOT’s noise policy is governed by Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) Noise Abatement Criteria (NAC). A traffic noise impact is officially identified when predicted levels equal or exceed 66 decibels (dBA) for residential areas. Mitigation measures like sound walls must meet "feasibility" (achieving a 5 \text{ dBA} reduction) and "reasonableness" (cost-effectiveness and public preference) criteria.

## Economic and Logistical Implications: The Inland Port Nexus
The relationship between SR-177 and the Western Weber Inland Port remains a significant point of contention. While UDOT frames the road as a benefit for commuters, the design's ability to handle large truck traffic makes it an ideal conduit for industrial freight. Local residents have expressed concern that the project will funnel semitruck traffic through quiet neighborhoods like Hooper.
## Local Governance and Implementation: West Haven City Actions
West Haven City has authorized several resolutions in 2026 to prepare for the corridor's arrival. Most notably, Resolution 16-2026 awarded the bid for the 5100 West roadway widening project to support future feeder traffic. The city’s economic plan also focuses on capturing sales tax revenue from growth stimulated by the highway.
## Legal Framework for Corridor Preservation and Eminent Domain
Utah’s Corridor Preservation statutes allow for "advance acquisition" of rights-of-way. Under Utah Code 72-5-407, corridor preservation is explicitly a voluntary process; UDOT cannot use eminent domain for preservation on unfunded projects. Eminent domain authority is only gained once the project enters the funded construction phase. In some cases, land is secured through developer dedication agreements, such as the 300-foot wide freeway parcel provided in the Westbridge Meadows development.
## Financial Context and Chronological Forecast
The extension from 1800 North to 4000 South remains currently unfunded for construction, though Phase 2 (Davis County) construction is launching in Summer 2026. Following the completion of the Weber County SES in late 2026, the project would typically enter a two-year design phase. Groundbreaking in Weber County could theoretically occur as early as 2030 if funding is secured, while the full corridor north to Box Elder County remains a long-term goal (2043–2050).

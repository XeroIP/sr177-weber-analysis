Section 01 — Strategic Planning

Planning Framework & Regional Context

Overview

Regional Mandates and Growth Pressure

The planning context for the SR-177 extension is governed by a tiered hierarchy of state and regional mandates, primarily channeled through UDOT and the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC). The overarching objective is to accommodate a projected population surge in western Davis and Weber counties, where the number of households is anticipated to increase by 74% by 2050. This demographic pressure necessitates a high-capacity, limited-access arterial capable of diverting traffic from the increasingly saturated Interstate 15 corridor.

The integration of SR-177 into the WFRC 2023–2050 Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) ensures the project aligns with the "Wasatch Choice Vision," which coordinates transportation infrastructure with land-use decisions and economic development strategies. For West Haven and Hooper, the highway is not merely a transit route but a catalyst for high-density residential rezoning and industrial expansion — specifically in relation to the Western Weber Inland Port project area.

Commuter growth projections (UDOT): Daily commuter trips from western Weber County to Davis County are expected to grow from ~9,250 today to 16,210 by 2050. Trips to Salt Lake County will increase from 2,310 to 5,370. Over 40% of workers in western Weber County currently commute to other counties.

The SES Process

State Environmental Study: Current Status

The transition of SR-177 into Weber County is currently managed through a State Environmental Study (SES) — a rigorous analytical process designed to evaluate the physical, biological, and social impacts of the proposed extension. As of Spring 2026, the study has moved from the initial scoping phase into the Alternatives Development and Evaluation stage.

This stage is critical for property owners near 5100 West and 3800 South, as it determines the precise alignment of the "Build Alternative" and the configuration of the 4000 South terminus. City officials have confirmed that UDOT has settled on an alignment along 5100 West, where the existing road will likely be converted to function as a frontage road for the corridor.

Important caveat: No preferred alignment has been officially published by UDOT as of April 2026. The SES is still in the alternatives evaluation phase. "Settled on" language from local officials should be treated as indicative, not definitive, until UDOT publishes formal alignment maps.

The study evaluates the intersection of the highway footprint with existing residential neighborhoods and the agricultural heritage of the Hooper area. The 2026 planning cycle is being executed with a 250-foot standard footprint, designed to accommodate four travel lanes separated by a median and flanked by multi-use trails — an upgrade from the more modest 2017 EIS configuration.

Section 02 — Property Impact

Property Impacts & Land Use Transformations

Your Property

5100 West near 3800 South — Scenario Analysis

Your address on the east side of 5100 West near 3800 South places you at the eastern boundary of the current study corridor. The outcome for your property depends entirely on which side of 5100 West the final alignment centerline falls — a decision that will not be made until UDOT publishes its Preferred Alternative, expected sometime in the Fall 2026 Draft SES.

Scenario A — Highest Risk

Centerline East of 5100 W

If the alignment shifts east of 5100 West, properties on the east side face direct ROW acquisition. At a 250-foot footprint, a centerline 125 feet east of 5100 W would bring the western ROW boundary to 5100 W itself. A partial or full take becomes likely.

Scenario B — Moderate Risk

Centerline On 5100 W

If the centerline sits directly on 5100 West, the 125-foot eastern ROW edge lands approximately 125 feet east — likely clipping or adjoining the front setback of east-side properties. Partial take for ROW or frontage road conversion is possible.

Scenario C — Indirect Impact

Centerline West of 5100 W

If the alignment moves toward 5500 West, your property avoids direct ROW take. However, you would likely be within the noise impact evaluation zone (~500 feet from centerline) and become a candidate for sound wall mitigation under FHWA criteria.

Scenario D — Frontage Road

5100 W Converted to Frontage Road

Local officials have indicated 5100 West may be repurposed as a frontage road. Under this scenario, the road in front of your property changes character from a neighborhood arterial to a controlled-access service road — affecting access patterns and property use.

Critical calibration: As of April 2026, no ROW boundary has been formally drawn. UDOT's Corridor Preservation Program has been acquiring properties in western Weber County for years, but no specific parcel data near 3800 South has been publicly released. The scenarios above are based on the 250-foot standard footprint and the 5100 West study corridor designation — not a finalized alignment map.

Broader Impact Data

Residential & Agricultural Displacement

The transition from a two-lane to a four-lane configuration has historically increased impacts on residential and agricultural parcels. Re-evaluations conducted in 2024–2025 for the Davis County Phase 2 extension revealed that design refinements resulted in an expanded project footprint compared to 2017 estimates. These figures provide the best available analog for what the Weber County segment may produce.

Relocation assistance is a primary component of UDOT's acquisition strategy, providing just compensation based on fair market value. These figures are from the Phase 2 Davis County re-evaluation and serve as the closest historical analog for what may occur in Weber County.

Corridor Preservation

Advance Acquisition Program

UDOT's Corridor Preservation Program has been acquiring properties in western Weber County for years in anticipation of eventual construction. Under Utah Code 72-5-407, this is explicitly a voluntary process — UDOT cannot use eminent domain for corridor preservation on an unfunded project.

In some cases, land has been secured through developer dedication agreements. An example: the Westbridge Meadows development provided a 300-foot wide freeway parcel as part of its development agreement with Weber County — wider than the 250-foot standard footprint, suggesting local planning may be accounting for a larger envelope than the standard specification.

What to do now: Contact UDOT Region 1 and request to be added to the SR-177 SES notification list at udotinput.utah.gov/sr177extension. When alignment maps are published during the Draft SES phase (expected Fall 2026), you can cross-reference your parcel against the formal ROW boundary for the first time.

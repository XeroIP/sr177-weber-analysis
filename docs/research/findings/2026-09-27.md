## Executive summary

**Question 1: Are other routes being considered besides 5100 West to about 3000 South?**
Yes, but mainly **north of 4000 South**, which is outside the current UDOT State Environmental Study (SES).

- **South of 4000 South (the current SES, 1800 North → 4000 South):** Every current source points to 5100 West. UDOT's study page says the corridor "follows 5100 W in Hooper." On March 4, 2026, West Haven's city manager told the council that "UDOT has settled on an alignment on 5100 W and it will act as a frontage road." No competing alignment for this segment was found in any 2025–2026 source. (Official record.)
- **"5100 West to about 3000 South" matches the adopted long-range line.** WFRC's 2023–2050 RTP data, Weber County's 2021 corridor-preservation exhibit, and the 2009 North Legacy study all run the corridor north on 5100 West to about 3000–3300 South. From there it curves northwest across the Weber River to about 6500–6700 West, runs north on the west side of Plain City, and then turns east to I-15 near the Box Elder County line. (Official record / official planning data.)
- **New in 2026: UDOT Region 1 reportedly prefers a different, "eastern" alignment north of West Haven.** Weber County Commissioner Gage Froerer said this at a joint County Commission/Planning Commission meeting on March 3, 2026. By Aug 4, 2026, county planning staff were describing UDOT's inclination as "a more northerly route" and presenting a counter-alignment that curves around state-owned wildlife land and wetlands. The goal was to "position the western corridor as the preferred alternative when UDOT eventually conducts a formal study." (Official record: county minutes. The exact UDOT "eastern" line has not been published.)
- **UDOT's consultant appears to be screening the northern segment now.** HDR, an SR-177 consultant, owns a public ArcGIS web map titled **"SR-177 Level 2 Screening"** (created Aug 24, 2026; modified Sept 8, 2026). Its study-area polygon is named **"SR177 Phase 4"** and covers a band from 4000 South at 5100 West, widening to roughly 4700–4800 West through 6700 West, then running northeast into South Willard in Box Elder County. The map layers are environmental constraints: wildlife management areas, an LWCF-funded park, wetlands, floodplains and agricultural protection areas. No alternative lines are published in the map. (Working GIS, unofficial. The claim that a formal Phase 4 screening is underway is an **inference**. UDOT has not announced a Phase 4 study.)
- **Other corridors on the books:** Weber County's general plan also preserves an **8300 West corridor** (at least 270 ft) for a possible future freeway "loop" to and from the North Legacy/SR-177 corridor to serve the inland-port area. The Westbridge Meadows agreement (about 1400 South, 7500 West) requires a 300-ft "West Weber Corridor" freeway parcel and a 200-ft "byway" parcel. (Official record.)
- **Historical alternatives that could resurface:** the Rocky Mountain Power "power line corridor" (about 3100 West), the most popular option with the public in 2007 and rejected in 2008–09; 4700 West (the 2003 county plan and the 2001 WFRC preferred alignment); a far-west alignment along 6500 West through Hooper (WDC EIS preliminary Alternative 12A); a Midland Drive connection in Roy and a Hooper Canal alignment (public suggestions in the 2010 WDC EIS); an 800-ft eastward shift through West Warren (6500 → 6300 West); and widening I-15 instead. (Official record, historical.)

**Question 2: Are Utah politicians pushing a different route through Weber County?**
- **Yes, at the county level, and they are pushing to keep the current western line, not to move it east.** All three Weber County Commissioners are involved:
  - Gage Froerer, a former Utah House member (2007–2018).
  - Jim Harvey.
  - Sharon Bolos.
  On June 29, 2026 the commissioners approved a staff plan to gather stakeholder signatures and send a letter to UDOT and state transportation officials "regarding keeping the West Weber freeway west of the river." Developers, landowners, and the Western Weber Planning Commission reportedly agreed. (Official record.)
- **UDOT Region 1 is the party reported to favor the different (eastern) route.** UDOT is an agency, not an elected body.
- **No state legislator was found publicly pushing a different Weber County route.** House Speaker Mike Schultz (R-Hooper) has publicly praised the corridor ("a game-changer for Davis and Weber counties," May 2021), but no route-specific position was found. No evidence was found that any legislator signed the county's 2026 letter. That is **unknown**, not confirmed absent.
- **Local mayors:** West Haven Mayor Rob Vanderwood is supportive and engaged. Hooper Mayor Sheri Bingham reportedly said "This road has been planned for many years." That quote is **second-hand**, from a letter to the editor.

**Stale or incorrect site claims found (see below):**
- `phase-4-longrange.njk` says no Phase 4 alignments or studies exist. That is contradicted by the 2009 study, WFRC data, 2026 county minutes, and consultant GIS.
- The `whc-warnke-minutes` citation dates the "UDOT has settled on 5100 W" statement to April 15, 2026. It is actually in the **March 4, 2026** minutes.

**What the "eastern alignment" actually is: not published.** Rumor / inference level only:
- *Inference:* The county's defensive framing ("keep it west of the river," "more northerly route") suggests UDOT's preferred line would **not** veer northwest at about 3000 South. Instead it would continue farther north on or near 5100 West, or a nearby north-south line east of the Weber River, before heading to I-15. That would put it through the 5100 West / 4700 West area of West Weber / Taylor rather than through the Reese / West Warren / 6700 West farm and wetland area.

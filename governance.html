Section 06 — Governance & Legal

Governance, Legal Framework & Your Rights

West Haven City Actions

Local Government Resolutions (2026)

West Haven City has authorized several resolutions in 2026 that directly reflect the city's preparation for the SR-177 corridor's arrival. These actions demonstrate that local government is actively planning around the highway's eventual construction — even though no construction timeline exists.

  • Resolution 09-2026 Adoption of Professional Services for Master Park Plan — leveraging planned trail infrastructure from the SR-177 corridor to enhance local recreational connectivity.
  • Resolution 15-2026 Construction authorization on 3600 South — road improvements in the project area reflecting anticipated traffic pattern changes.
  • Resolution 16-2026 Award of bid for the 5100 West roadway widening project. This is the most direct signal of alignment: the city is widening 5100 West specifically to support future "feeder traffic" from SR-177. West Haven's economic plan also focuses on capturing sales tax revenue from growth stimulated by the highway.
  • April 15, 2026 — City Council Meeting Shawn Warnke (UDOT representative) presented on SR-177 alignment to the West Haven City Council. Meeting minutes are publicly available through the Utah Public Notice website (utah.gov/pmn).

What Resolution 16-2026 tells you: West Haven widening 5100 West — the street your property fronts — is a strong indicator that the city and UDOT expect 5100 West to function as a frontage road or significant arterial connector once SR-177 is built. This widening project may directly affect your property's front setback and street character regardless of the final highway alignment.

Legal Framework

Eminent Domain and Corridor Preservation Law

Understanding the distinction between corridor preservation and eminent domain is critical for property owners in the study area.

Corridor Preservation (Pre-Funding)

Under Utah Code 72-5-407, corridor preservation is explicitly a voluntary process. UDOT cannot use eminent domain to acquire property for corridor preservation on an unfunded project. When UDOT approaches a property owner under this program, the sale is optional — you are not required to sell.

However, if you do sell to UDOT under the Corridor Preservation Program, you typically receive fair market value based on a certified appraisal. Some owners find this advantageous if they wish to sell before the project creates uncertainty in the private market.

Eminent Domain (Post-Funding)

Once the Weber County SR-177 extension enters the funded construction phase, UDOT gains eminent domain authority. At that point, if your property is within the final ROW boundary, acquisition becomes mandatory. You retain the right to:

  • Receive a certified appraisal from UDOT
  • Commission your own independent appraisal at UDOT's expense
  • Negotiate the compensation amount
  • Contest the acquisition in court if you believe compensation is inadequate (inverse condemnation)
  • Receive relocation assistance if you must vacate the property

Current status: Because the Weber County extension is unfunded, eminent domain authority does not currently exist. UDOT can only acquire property in this corridor through voluntary transactions under the Corridor Preservation Program. This will change when and if construction funding is allocated.

How to Participate

Monitor the SES and Protect Your Interests

The most important actions you can take right now:

  1. Register for SES updates at udotinput.utah.gov/sr177extension to receive notification when alignment maps are published.
  2. Submit formal public comment during the Draft SES comment period (expected Fall 2026). Written comments become part of the official project record.
  3. Attend public hearings when scheduled. UDOT is required to hold a public hearing during the Draft SES phase.
  4. Review West Haven City Council minutes at utah.gov/pmn for ongoing corridor-related resolutions and presentations.
  5. Consult a property attorney familiar with Utah eminent domain law if UDOT's Corridor Preservation Program contacts you about acquiring your property or an easement.
